US sterile injectable shortages persist; South African public clinics short of psychiatric drugs

Why it's at this level: The FDA's shortage database still lists dozens of current shortages, mostly sterile injectables including cytarabine and irinotecan (FDA). In South Africa, public clinics are short of psychiatric and other medicines, and some patients are buying them privately, TimesLIVE reported on 6 Nov. No widespread rationing or cancelled treatment across major health systems, and no new UK Serious Shortage Protocol for a widely used medicine, was found as of 6 Nov.

What this means for you: If your medicine is out of stock, your pharmacist or prescriber can advise on alternatives; in the UK, NHS guidance covers what to do. Patients on sterile injectables and psychiatric drugs are most exposed.

Why it matters: Shipping disruption, factory failures and export curbs can leave essential medicines short, as with US IV fluids after Hurricane Helene in 2024.
What moves it: Manufacturing concentrated in India and China, shipping, raw materials, demand spikes, regulation.

Rubric — Alert: Shortages forcing widespread rationing or cancelled treatment across major health systems. | Watch: A shortage of an essential medicine or supply across several countries, or a UK Serious Shortage Protocol for a widely used medicine. | Normal: No widespread shortages of essential medicines in major health systems.
