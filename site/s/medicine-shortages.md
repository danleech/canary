UK pharmacists say shortages hit up to 6.4m items a month; US active shortages steady at 225 in Q3

Why it's at this level: On 17 Nov the National Pharmacy Association estimated shortages could affect up to 6.4 million prescription items a month in 2026; price concessions hit a record 254 in July and 245 in October (Pharmaceutical Journal). In the US, ASHP counted 225 active shortages in the third quarter, with chemotherapy shortages worsening after disruption at a Pfizer plant in India (Becker's Hospital Review, 20 Oct). Shortages span several countries but no widespread rationing across major health systems was found on 21 Nov, so Watch holds.

What this means for you: If a prescribed medicine is out of stock, your pharmacist can advise on alternatives or a Serious Shortage Protocol substitute. Follow NHS guidance and do not stop treatment without speaking to a clinician.

Why it matters: Shipping disruption, factory failures and export curbs can leave essential medicines short, as with US IV fluids after Hurricane Helene in 2024.
What moves it: Manufacturing concentrated in India and China, shipping, raw materials, demand spikes, regulation.
