UK pharmacists say shortages hit up to 6.4m items a month; US chemotherapy shortages worsened in Q3

Why it's at this level: On 17 Nov the National Pharmacy Association estimated shortages could affect up to 6.4 million prescription items a month in 2026; price concessions hit a record 254 in July and 245 in October (Pharmaceutical Journal). In the US, ASHP counted 225 active shortages in the third quarter, with chemotherapy shortages rising after flooding at a Pfizer plant in India (Becker's Hospital Review, 20 Oct). Shortages span several countries but no widespread rationing across major health systems was found on 20 Nov, so Watch holds.

What this means for you: Patients on affected medicines may face delays or substitutes at the pharmacy. Speak to your pharmacist or prescriber; do not stop treatment without advice.

Why it matters: Shipping disruption, factory failures and export curbs can leave essential medicines short, as with US IV fluids after Hurricane Helene in 2024.
What moves it: Manufacturing concentrated in India and China, shipping, raw materials, demand spikes, regulation.

Rubric — Alert: Shortages forcing widespread rationing or cancelled treatment across major health systems. | Watch: A shortage of an essential medicine or supply across several countries, or a UK Serious Shortage Protocol for a widely used medicine. | Normal: No widespread shortages of essential medicines in major health systems.
