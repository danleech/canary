Ofgem confirms the typical dual-fuel cap rises 26% to £2,166 a year from 1 January on higher wholesale gas prices

Why it's at this level: Ofgem announced on 25 Nov that the cap for a typical dual-fuel household paying by Direct Debit will rise 26% to £2,166 a year from 1 Jan to 31 Mar 2027, up from £1,723 (Ofgem). It attributes the rise to higher wholesale gas prices linked to the war in the Middle East, partly offset by the removal of VAT from electricity bills until 30 Jun 2027. The confirmed rise exceeds the 20% Alert line, although the cap level stays below £2,200.

What this means for you: Households on standard variable tariffs face higher bills from 1 January. Those struggling to pay can find support options in Ofgem's energy advice pages.

Why it matters: The Ofgem cap sets bills for most UK households — a direct cost-of-living signal.
What moves it: Wholesale gas and power prices, network costs, policy levies.
