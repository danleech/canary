Ofgem's price cap for a typical dual-fuel home rises 26% to £2,166 a year from 1 January on higher gas prices

Why it's at this level: Ofgem announced on 25 Nov that the cap for a typical dual-fuel household paying by Direct Debit will rise 26% to £2,166 a year for 1 Jan to 31 Mar 2027, up from £1,723 (Ofgem). Ofgem attributes the rise to the Middle East conflict lifting international gas prices; Cornwall Insight had forecast the same level (ITV News, 18 Nov). The 26% rise exceeds the 20% Alert line, although the cap stays below £2,200.

What this means for you: Households on standard variable tariffs face higher bills from 1 January, with gas-heated homes most exposed. Ofgem's website sets out the cap and where to find help with energy bills.

Why it matters: The Ofgem cap sets bills for most UK households — a direct cost-of-living signal.
What moves it: Wholesale gas and power prices, network costs, policy levies.
