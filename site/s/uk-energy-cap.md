The typical dual-fuel energy price cap is set to rise 26% to about £2,166 a year from 1 January on higher gas prices

Why it's at this level: Cornwall Insight forecast on 18 Nov that the typical dual-fuel cap will rise 26%, or £443, to £2,166 a year for 1 Jan to 31 Mar 2027, up from the current £1,723 (City AM; ITV News). The rise is attributed to higher international gas prices linked to the Middle East conflict. A rise of 26% exceeds the 20% Alert line, although the cap level itself stays below £2,200. Searches on 28 Nov found no change to that picture.

What this means for you: Households on standard variable tariffs face higher bills from 1 January, with the largest effect on those in poorly insulated homes or on low incomes. Ofgem's website explains how the cap works and where to find help with bills.

Why it matters: The Ofgem cap sets bills for most UK households — a direct cost-of-living signal.
What moves it: Wholesale gas and power prices, network costs, policy levies.
