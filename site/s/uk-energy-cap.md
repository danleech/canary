Ofgem's price cap for a typical dual-fuel home rises 26% to £2,166 a year from 1 January on higher wholesale gas

Why it's at this level: Ofgem announced on 25 Nov that the cap for a typical dual-fuel household paying by Direct Debit will rise 26% to £2,166 a year from 1 Jan to 31 Mar 2027, up from £1,723, in line with Cornwall Insight's forecast (Ofgem; IBTimes). The rise reflects higher wholesale gas prices linked to the Middle East war, partly offset by removal of VAT on electricity bills until 30 Jun 2027. The rise exceeds the 20% Alert line, although the cap stays below £2,200.

What this means for you: Ofgem's website explains what the cap means for your bills; people struggling to pay can contact their supplier about support schemes and the government's Household Energy Payment.

Why it matters: The Ofgem cap sets bills for most UK households — a direct cost-of-living signal.
What moves it: Wholesale gas and power prices, network costs, policy levies.
