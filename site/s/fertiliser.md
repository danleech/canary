Why it's at this level: US retail urea averaged $657 a tonne in the week of 9–13 Nov, slightly below mid-October and well under the $866 peak of late April, though 10% higher than a year earlier (DTN, 18 Nov). Egyptian granular urea export prices reached $535 a tonne after gas deliveries to producers were cut from 1 Nov, about 17% above September (UkrAgroConsult). DTN cites threats to shipping through the Strait of Hormuz, which carries Gulf nitrogen exports; searches on 28 Nov found no urea rise above 30% in three months and no export bans by several major exporters.

What this means for you: Farmers buying nitrogen for spring planting face prices above last year's; higher fertiliser costs tend to feed into crop prices in the following season. Gulf-dependent importers in South Asia and Africa are most exposed to Hormuz disruption.

Why it matters: Fertiliser prices lead food prices by months; much of the world's urea and ammonia is exported from Russia, the Gulf and China.
What moves it: Gas prices, Gulf shipping, export bans and quotas, sanctions, planting seasons.
