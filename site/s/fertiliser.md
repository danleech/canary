Why it's at this level: IFDC said on 24 Nov that urea prices have been broadly stable since a correction in October, with granular urea sold at $500 a tonne FOB Egypt in the week to 20 Nov, below the $535 quoted early in November (EnterpriseAM). Nine urea-laden vessels crossed the Strait of Hormuz in November, the most since June, and Egyptian producers cut output by 20–33% after gas supply cuts (IFDC). Gulf exports still rely on an impeded Hormuz route, so Watch holds; no export bans by several major exporters were found.

What this means for you: Farmers face higher fertiliser costs than before the Gulf war, which can feed into food prices over later seasons. Prices could move sharply either way depending on events in the Gulf.

Why it matters: Fertiliser prices lead food prices by months; much of the world's urea and ammonia is exported from Russia, the Gulf and China.
What moves it: Gas prices, Gulf shipping, export bans and quotas, sanctions, planting seasons.
