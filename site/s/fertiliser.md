Hormuz closure has stranded Gulf urea exports; prices up but below April peak

Why it's at this level: No non-Iranian granular urea has cleared Hormuz since mid-October, and Argus counts at least 21 vessels with 919,000 tonnes of urea held in the Gulf, against normal exports of about 1Mt a month (28 Oct). Middle East urea was $447.50/t on 27 Oct, well below the April peak of about $780.

What this means for you: Higher fertiliser costs tend to feed into food prices over the following seasons, hitting import-dependent farmers and poorer countries first.

Why it matters: Fertiliser prices lead food prices by months; much of the world's urea and ammonia is exported from Russia, the Gulf and China.
What moves it: Gas prices, Gulf shipping, export bans and quotas, sanctions, planting seasons.

Rubric — Alert: Urea up more than 60% in three months, several major exporters restricting at once, or a key export route closed. | Watch: Urea up more than 30% in three months, or a major exporter restricts exports. | Normal: Urea and ammonia prices within their normal range; no major export restrictions.
