Redemption requests at most nontraded BDCs stay above their 5% quarterly cap; no wave of gates or forced sales

Why it's at this level: Fitch found that 13 of 15 perpetual nontraded BDCs received third-quarter redemption requests averaging 9.8% of shares, down from 10.3% in the second quarter but well above their 5% quarterly caps; Blue Owl Technology Income Corp faced requests for 39% (InvestmentNews, 13 Oct). Fitch warned in March that more semi-liquid funds may hold redemptions to their caps if the exit rush continues (Creditflux). Searches on 22 Nov found no simultaneous gating by several large funds, no forced asset sales and no losses spreading to banks or insurers, so Watch holds.

What this means for you: Investors in semi-liquid private credit funds may find withdrawals limited to a share of the fund each quarter. Wider stress would tend to show first in tighter lending to mid-sized companies.

Why it matters: Private credit has grown fast outside bank regulation; its losses surface late and can spill into banks, insurers and pension funds.
What moves it: Defaults among leveraged mid-sized borrowers, payment-in-kind interest, fund redemption requests, bank lending to funds.

Rubric — Alert: Several large funds gate at once, forced asset sales, or losses spreading to banks or insurers. | Watch: A large fund limits redemptions, listed BDCs trade at wide discounts to NAV, or a cluster of borrower defaults with heavy losses. | Normal: No notable fund gates, defaults or markdowns beyond the routine.
