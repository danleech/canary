Why it's at this level: Fitch data reported on 17 Nov show the trailing 12-month US private credit default rate eased to 6.2% in October from 6.3% in September, with 10.7% for borrowers with EBITDA under $25m and 9.8% in industrials (Investment Executive). Fitch said in October that nontraded BDCs faced third-quarter redemption requests averaging 9.8% of shares against a 5% quarterly cap (InvestmentNews). Searches on 19 Nov found no simultaneous gating by several large funds, forced asset sales or losses spreading to banks or insurers, so Watch holds.

What this means for you: Mainly affects holders of nontraded BDCs and private credit funds, whose withdrawals are capped at 5% a quarter, and mid-sized firms that borrow from these lenders. Defaults tend to show up first in smaller and industrial borrowers.

Why it matters: Private credit has grown fast outside bank regulation; its losses surface late and can spill into banks, insurers and pension funds.
What moves it: Defaults among leveraged mid-sized borrowers, payment-in-kind interest, fund redemption requests, bank lending to funds.
