Semi-liquid credit funds keep 5% redemption caps and defaults are rising; no forced sales or bank losses found

Why it's at this level: Fitch said on 13 Oct that redemption requests at 13 of 15 perpetual nontraded BDCs averaged 9.8% of shares in the third quarter, against caps of 5% a quarter (InvestmentNews). KBRA reported the largest quarterly jump in private credit defaults on 5 Nov (Bloomberg), and Australia's regulator ASIC said on 10 Nov that opacity and weak disclosure persist and issued new interim stop orders (ABC). Searches on 15 Nov found no simultaneous wave of gates, forced asset sales or losses spreading to banks or insurers, so Watch holds.

What this means for you: Investors in semi-liquid private credit funds and some pension schemes are exposed to redemption limits, which can delay withdrawals. Bank deposits are not directly affected by these funds.

Why it matters: Private credit has grown fast outside bank regulation; its losses surface late and can spill into banks, insurers and pension funds.
What moves it: Defaults among leveraged mid-sized borrowers, payment-in-kind interest, fund redemption requests, bank lending to funds.

Rubric — Alert: Several large funds gate at once, forced asset sales, or losses spreading to banks or insurers. | Watch: A large fund limits redemptions, listed BDCs trade at wide discounts to NAV, or a cluster of borrower defaults with heavy losses. | Normal: No notable fund gates, defaults or markdowns beyond the routine.
