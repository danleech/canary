Redemption requests at most nontraded BDCs remain above their 5% quarterly caps; no wave of gates or forced sales found

Why it's at this level: Fitch found that 13 of 15 perpetual nontraded BDCs received third-quarter redemption requests averaging 9.8% of shares, down from 10.3% in the second quarter but well above their 5% quarterly caps, with Blue Owl Technology Income Corp facing requests for 39% (InvestmentNews, 13 Oct). Searches on 23 Nov found no new simultaneous gating by several large funds, no forced asset sales and no losses spreading to banks or insurers, so Watch holds.

What this means for you: Investors in semi-liquid private credit funds may find withdrawals limited to each fund's quarterly cap and paid pro rata. Fund documents set out how gates work.

Why it matters: Private credit has grown fast outside bank regulation; its losses surface late and can spill into banks, insurers and pension funds.
What moves it: Defaults among leveraged mid-sized borrowers, payment-in-kind interest, fund redemption requests, bank lending to funds.

Rubric — Alert: Several large funds gate at once, forced asset sales, or losses spreading to banks or insurers. | Watch: A large fund limits redemptions, listed BDCs trade at wide discounts to NAV, or a cluster of borrower defaults with heavy losses. | Normal: No notable fund gates, defaults or markdowns beyond the routine.
