Why it's at this level: Fitch found that 13 of 15 perpetual nontraded BDCs received third-quarter redemption requests averaging 9.8% of shares, above their usual 5% quarterly caps, with Blue Owl Technology Income Corp facing requests for 39% (InvestmentNews, 13 Oct). Client redemptions from nontraded BDCs reached $12.7bn in the first half of 2026, and managers hope to bring them below 5% of NAV by mid-2027 (InvestmentNews, 18 Nov). Searches on 24 Nov found no simultaneous gating by several large funds, forced sales or losses spreading to banks or insurers, so Watch holds.

What this means for you: People holding nontraded BDCs or semi-liquid private credit funds may find withdrawals limited to a share of what they request each quarter. Wider spillover to banks or insurers has not been reported.

Why it matters: Private credit has grown fast outside bank regulation; its losses surface late and can spill into banks, insurers and pension funds.
What moves it: Defaults among leveraged mid-sized borrowers, payment-in-kind interest, fund redemption requests, bank lending to funds.

Rubric — Alert: Several large funds gate at once, forced asset sales, or losses spreading to banks or insurers. | Watch: A large fund limits redemptions, listed BDCs trade at wide discounts to NAV, or a cluster of borrower defaults with heavy losses. | Normal: No notable fund gates, defaults or markdowns beyond the routine.
