Why it's at this level: The FAO Food Price Index averaged 133.9 points in October, published on 6 Nov, down 1.2% from September and 5.9% above a year earlier (FAO). Cereals fell 4.5% and meat and dairy eased, while sugar rose 6.6% for a fourth monthly gain to its highest since December 2024. The annual rise meets the Watch line; the index is below the 135 Alert line and no new staple export bans by several major exporters were found as of 13 Nov.

What this means for you: Households and food importers face food costs higher than a year ago, with sugar and vegetable oils rising fastest. Price moves on world markets usually reach shop prices with a lag of several months.

Why it matters: Food prices drive inflation, hunger and political unrest; poorer countries spend up to half of income on food.
What moves it: Harvests, weather, fertiliser and energy costs, export bans, conflict in breadbaskets.
