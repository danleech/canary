Why it's at this level: The FAO Food Price Index rose 1.5% in September to 136.0, 5.8% above a year earlier and above the 135 Alert line (published 8 Oct). Cereals rose 5.1% and sugar 6.1%, driven by Black Sea logistics, dry weather and Hormuz shipping uncertainty. The October figure is due in the first week of November; no new reading as of 2 Nov.

What this means for you: Food inflation is likely to stay elevated. Import-dependent poorer countries are most exposed.

Why it matters: Food prices drive inflation, hunger and political unrest; poorer countries spend up to half of income on food.
What moves it: Harvests, weather, fertiliser and energy costs, export bans, conflict in breadbaskets.
