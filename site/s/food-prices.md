Why it's at this level: The FAO Food Price Index averaged 133.9 points in October, down 1.2% on September but 5.9% above October 2025 (FAO, 6 Nov). Sugar rose 6.6% for a fourth monthly rise while cereals fell 4.5%. The annual rise meets the Watch line and the index stays below 135; Indian exporters are bracing for possible curbs on broken rice (S&P Global), but searches on 21 Nov found no staple export bans by several major exporters. What this means for you: Households in import-dependent countries and lower-income consumers are most exposed to higher food costs; sugar-heavy products are rising fastest.

Why it matters: Food prices drive inflation, hunger and political unrest; poorer countries spend up to half of income on food.
What moves it: Harvests, weather, fertiliser and energy costs, export bans, conflict in breadbaskets.
