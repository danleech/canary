Why it's at this level: The FAO Food Price Index averaged 133.9 points in October, down 1.2% on September but 5.9% above October 2025 (FAO; Food Ingredients First). Sugar rose 6.6% for a fourth monthly rise while cereals fell 4.5%. The annual rise meets the Watch line and the index stays below 135; searches on 20 Nov found no staple export bans by several major exporters, and the next release is due on 4 Dec.

What this means for you: Global food costs are higher than a year ago, which tends to feed into supermarket prices with a lag and hits import-dependent countries hardest.

Why it matters: Food prices drive inflation, hunger and political unrest; poorer countries spend up to half of income on food.
What moves it: Harvests, weather, fertiliser and energy costs, export bans, conflict in breadbaskets.
