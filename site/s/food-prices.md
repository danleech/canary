Why it's at this level: The FAO Food Price Index averaged 133.9 points in October, down 1.2% on September but 5.9% above October 2025 (FAO; Food Ingredients First, 9 Nov). Sugar rose 6.6% on the month to its highest since December 2024, while cereals fell 4.5%. The annual rise meets the Watch line and the index stays below 135; searches on 15 Nov found India's rice exporters bracing for possible curbs on broken rice, but no staple export bans by several major exporters.

What this means for you: Households in import-dependent countries and lower-income households spend the largest share on food and are most exposed. In the UK, higher world prices tend to reach shop shelves over several months.

Why it matters: Food prices drive inflation, hunger and political unrest; poorer countries spend up to half of income on food.
What moves it: Harvests, weather, fertiliser and energy costs, export bans, conflict in breadbaskets.
