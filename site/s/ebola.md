DR Congo's Ebola outbreak passed 11,200 cases and 5,500 deaths by 15 Nov; Africa CDC says it is not under control

Why it's at this level: Africa CDC said on 15 Nov that government data show more than 11,200 cases and 5,500 deaths in DR Congo, in eight of 26 provinces, and that over 80% of new cases come from outside contact lists (Al Jazeera). Africanews reported on 12 Nov that conflict is hampering the response. There is no licensed vaccine for the Bundibugyo strain; WHO-approved trials are expected to report in early 2027. Cases far exceed the 1,000 Alert line.

What this means for you: Travellers should follow FCDO and UKHSA advice for DR Congo and neighbouring countries. Risk to the UK public remains very low.

Why it matters: Ebola, Marburg and similar viruses kill a large share of those infected; outbreaks that reach cities or cross borders can overwhelm health systems.
What moves it: Surveillance and contact tracing, vaccine availability (none licensed for some strains), conflict, cross-border travel, health-worker infections.
