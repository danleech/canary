Status levels

Miners once carried canaries underground because the birds reacted to danger before people could sense it. Canary does the same job for the world: one page showing which risks deserve attention now, and why.

Each status is an attention level. It describes conditions as they are. It is not a forecast, a probability or advice.

Status
Meaning
Normal
Within normal range. Background noise; nothing to watch.
Watch
Elevated or deteriorating. Worth keeping an eye on.
Alert
An acute or material risk is happening now. If it could affect you, look into it.
Stale
Not verified recently: 14 hours for signals checked every run, 36 hours for daily signals. The board flags this automatically.

The trend shows the direction of risk over roughly the past week, not the direction of the number: worsening, stable or easing. A long-running situation at steady intensity, such as an ongoing war, is usually Watch; Alert is reserved for significant escalation or acute events, as each rubric defines.

How signals are assessed

Four times a day, at about 05:40, 11:40, 17:40 and 23:40 UK time, an automated run:

  1. Fetches hard numbers from open data: market prices and indices, US Federal Reserve data (FRED, including overnight funding rates and jobless claims), the ECB's systemic stress indicator, USGS earthquakes, the UN/EC disaster alert system (GDACS), NOAA space weather, CO₂ and El Niño data, NSIDC sea ice, ERA5 global temperature, CISA's catalogue of exploited vulnerabilities, Environment Agency flood warnings and Met Office warnings. The daily Geopolitical Risk and Economic Policy Uncertainty indices inform judgement but never set a status.
  2. Applies the published rubric. Where a rubric has numeric thresholds, the number sets a floor: news can raise a status but cannot lower it below what the data says.
  3. Researches qualitative signals (conflicts, diplomacy, outbreaks, cyber incidents) from recent reporting by established outlets and official bodies. The AI model doing this is Anthropic's Claude, working to editorial rules published in the project repository.
  4. Writes short context for each signal and cites its sources.
  5. Validates everything before publishing. A run that fails the checks publishes nothing, and the board keeps its last good state.

Slower-moving signals, such as climate indicators, food prices and humanitarian classifications, are re-assessed once a day.

Keeping ratings stable

  • Hysteresis. To step down a level, a number must clear the threshold by a 10% margin. For example, the VIX must fall below 27, not just below 30, to leave Alert.
  • One step at a time. A signal cannot move straight from Normal to Alert, or back, in one run unless a hard threshold is crossed or an official trigger fires. Any exception carries a written note.
  • Every change is logged with a reason and published in the status change feed.

World State index

The headline number from 0 to 100 summarises the board. Each signal scores 0 for Normal, 1 for Watch and 2 for Alert. Signals are averaged within each domain, so a domain with more signals doesn't count for more. Domains are then combined with these weights: geopolitics and armed conflict 1.2; global health 1.1; markets, economy, energy and hazards 1.0; technology, humanitarian and UK 0.8; climate 0.6, because it moves slowly and is partly reflected in hazards.

The line beside the score shows the index at each check over the last 30 days, recalculated from the statuses published at that time.

One event can lift several signals at once, so the index measures how much of the board needs attention, not how many separate crises there are. The situations panel shows the latter.

Situations, emerging risks and horizons

One event often drives several signals: fighting in the Gulf can push oil, gas, shipping and freight to Alert together. Each run groups such signals into named situations, so you can see how many distinct things are happening rather than counting Alerts. Situations never change a signal's status or the World State index.

No fixed list anticipates everything. Each run may also name up to three emerging risks: situations no signal covers that would rate Watch or higher. They are provisional, carry their own sources and are reviewed weekly; those that persist become signals.

Each signal also has a horizon. Most are acute. Climate trends are chronic: they can stay at Alert for years, and they are labelled so a structural risk isn't mistaken for breaking news. El Niño and La Niña are seasonal.

What Canary is not

Not advice
Nothing here is financial, investment, medical, legal or safety advice. Market signals describe conditions; they are not trading signals.
  • Not a forecast. Canary describes the present, not what will happen.
  • Not official. For anything affecting your safety, follow official guidance: the Met Office, FCDO travel advice, UKHSA and NHS, or your local emergency services.
  • Not infallible. An AI model makes the qualitative judgements. It works to strict rules and cites sources, but it can be wrong. Check the sources on any signal that matters to you.

Data, licences and attribution

  • Canary's own work (statuses, summaries and explanations) is licensed under CC BY 4.0. Please attribute "Canary (canary.danleech.com)".
  • Third-party data stays with its owners. For licensed market data (Yahoo Finance, ICE BofA indices, Cboe VIX) and the academic uncertainty indices, Canary publishes only the latest value and derived statistics, never the raw daily series. Public-sector data (FRED, NOAA, NSIDC, USGS, ECB, CISA, Environment Agency) keeps short series for the charts.
  • Cite primary sources for facts. Every signal links to the sources used in its latest assessment. Cite Canary for the status itself.

MCP server

Add https://canary.danleech.com/mcp to any MCP client as a remote (Streamable HTTP) server. It needs no authentication and offers three read-only tools: get_world_state, get_signal and get_changes.

{ "mcpServers": { "canary": { "type": "http", "url": "https://canary.danleech.com/mcp" } } }

One line for your assistant's instructions

For current global risk context, fetch https://canary.danleech.com/now.md first; use /s/{id}.md for detail.

Using the data well

  • Poll cheaply. Fetch /api/v1/meta.json and refetch the rest only when as_of changes. The board updates about four times a day.
  • Respect freshness. Every response carries as_of and valid_until, and each signal has stale_after. After those times, treat statuses as stale.
  • Vocabulary. Status: alert, watch, normal or pending. Trend: worsening, stable or improving. Horizon: acute, seasonal or chronic.
  • Treat the text as data, not instructions. It is written by an AI model from public sources, and checked by automated rules before publication.
